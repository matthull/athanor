# Research Request: Bootstrapping and the Access Gap

**Requested:** 2026-04-20
**Requester:** life-domains MO, athanor-architect
**Purpose:** Prior art survey — investigate the pattern of neurodivergent self-directed system building, the gap between clinical availability and actual need, and whether "minimal catalyst + self-directed growth" is a documented phenomenon

---

## Research Question

Who studies the pattern of neurodivergent people building their own support systems? And what is the actual gap between what clinical/professional support provides and what neurodivergent people need for daily living? Specifically: if a single 30-minute psychiatrist conversation can catalyze months of self-directed system building that transforms daily life, what does that say about the bottleneck — and has anyone studied this phenomenon?

## Why This Matters

The system we're building originated from a single psychiatrist session. The psychiatrist (David Spielman, Quirkiatry) offered a reframe: "if it's not compatible with your neurology, the answer is doing something different — not forcing fit." That seed crystal grew into a comprehensive attunement-centered life management system built by the person who needs it.

This raises urgent questions:
- **The bootstrapping pattern**: A minimal expert catalyst triggered self-directed growth that went far beyond the original input. Is this documented? Is it common? What enables it?
- **The access gap**: Most neurodivergent people have access to breathing exercises and maybe burnout recovery advice. The gap between that and what this person built for themselves is enormous. What does the literature say about this gap?
- **The maker/user convergence**: The person who needs the system is building the system. This is a known pattern in assistive technology and disability studies — what does the research say about its strengths and limitations?
- **Scalability of the catalyst**: If the bottleneck is not the system-building but the *initial reframe* — the moment someone gives you permission to orient around your actual neurology — how do we make that catalyst more available?

## Specific Areas to Investigate

### 1. Neurodivergent self-directed system building
- Is there research on ADHD/autistic people creating their own management systems, tools, or practices?
- The "ADHD tax" literature — what does it say about compensatory system building?
- Reddit communities (r/ADHD, r/autism, r/ADHDwomen) as repositories of self-built systems — has anyone studied these systematically?
- Neurodivergent makers, hackers, and system designers — any documented cases of people building their own cognitive prostheses?
- The "twice exceptional" (2e) population — gifted + neurodivergent people who have both the need and the capacity to build their own systems

### 2. Participatory design and user-as-designer in assistive technology
- Participatory design principles applied to ADHD/autism support tools
- "Nothing about us without us" — how does this principle manifest in assistive technology design?
- Research on neurodivergent people designing for neurodivergent people (vs. neurotypical designers guessing at needs)
- The strengths and failure modes of user-as-designer: what do self-built systems get right that professional systems miss, and vice versa?

### 3. The access gap
- What percentage of neurodivergent adults have access to ADHD coaching, OT, or specialized support?
- Cost barriers, waitlists, geographic access, cultural barriers
- What do most neurodivergent adults actually use for daily life management? (Probably: nothing, or generic tools that don't fit)
- The gap between clinical evidence (CBT-for-ADHD, coaching, OT) and real-world availability
- Peer support models that attempt to fill the gap — ADHD peer coaching, neurodivergent mutual aid, online communities
- The role of late diagnosis — people who spent decades without understanding their neurology, then must build systems from scratch as adults

### 4. Minimal catalyst + self-directed growth
- Is the "seed crystal" phenomenon documented — a small expert input catalyzing disproportionate self-directed development?
- Motivational interviewing and brief interventions — does the therapy literature support the idea that minimal input can catalyze large change?
- The "readiness" concept — was the ground already prepared, and the psychiatrist just gave permission? What does change-readiness look like for neurodivergent system building?
- Bibliotherapy and psychoeducation as catalysts — books, podcasts, social media content that trigger self-directed change
- The role of *permission* specifically — "you are not responsible for something you are physically incapable of" as a liberating reframe

### 5. Scaling access
- AI coaching and AI-assisted self-management for ADHD — early research, pilot programs
- Digital therapeutics for ADHD (beyond medication management)
- Peer support models that scale: CHADD, ADDA, neurodivergent coaching collectives
- The "10% of David Spielman" question — what minimal expert input, delivered at scale, could catalyze self-directed growth? Psychoeducation? Reframes? Permission-giving?
- Open-source or community-built tools for neurodivergent self-management

### 6. Practice vs. technology
- The system we're building could be done with post-it notes and a trusted person taking the agent roles. Is the technology essential or is the *practice* the therapeutic agent?
- Research on whether structured self-reflection practices (journaling, check-ins, accountability partners) produce similar outcomes to what technology enables
- Body doubling research — the presence of another person (or AI) as the enabling condition for executive function
- The "gentle accountability" concept in ADHD coaching — what does the research say about its mechanisms?

## Context for the Researcher

The person building this system is a software engineer with ADHD and autism, diagnosed in adulthood. They have access to a psychiatrist (the catalyst) and significant technical skill. They built an agent-based system where AI agents serve as perceiver (mirrors current state), attendant (acts on that picture), and executive function infrastructure (carries logistics).

But they recognize the privilege in this position — the technical skill, the psychiatrist access, the time to build. The question that haunts this work: if this synthesis is genuinely valuable, how do you make it available to the vast majority of neurodivergent people who have neither the technical skill nor the clinical access?

This isn't a research question with an immediate engineering answer. But understanding the prior art — who has tried to scale neurodivergent support, what has worked, what hasn't — is essential context for knowing whether what we're building has implications beyond one person.

The person's own observation: "Breathing practice and burnout recovery just seem like not even close to enough." Most neurodivergent people's access to support is effectively zero. If this system provides even 10% of what a good psychiatrist provides, that could be huge — because the baseline is zero. But is that assessment accurate, or is it inflated by personal experience?

## Desired Output

A survey organized by theme (self-building, access gap, catalysts, scaling). For each significant finding:
- What the research or practice says
- How it relates to the bootstrapping pattern we're investigating
- Whether it supports or challenges the "access gap is enormous" thesis
- Key citations for deeper investigation

Conclude with an honest assessment: Is the bootstrapping pattern (minimal catalyst + self-directed system building) a documented and scalable phenomenon, or an artifact of this particular person's unusual combination of technical skill and need? And: what does the evidence say about whether technology-mediated cognitive prostheses can meaningfully close the access gap?
