# Research Request: Apps and Consumer Tools for Neurodivergent Life Management

**Requested:** 2026-04-20
**Requester:** life-domains MO, athanor-architect
**Purpose:** Prior art survey — map the landscape of digital tools that attempt state-first or attunement-centered approaches for neurodivergent users

---

## Research Question

What apps, digital tools, and consumer products target neurodivergent life management beyond simple task managers? Especially interested in tools that take a state-first approach (check in with how you're doing before deciding what to do), sensory/regulatory tools, and anything attempting the attunement-to-action pipeline. What has been tried, what has succeeded, what has failed, and why?

## Why This Matters

We are building a system where the entry point is always interoceptive awareness ("how am I right now?") before action selection ("what should I do?"). Most ADHD/neurodivergent apps we're aware of are task managers with ADHD-friendly UX (Tiimo, Routinery, Goblin Tools) or habit trackers (Finch, Habitica). We want to know:

1. Has anyone shipped a consumer product built on the premise that *state awareness comes before task selection*?
2. What has the market tried and abandoned — the failures are as informative as the successes
3. Are there apps that attempt the full loop (check-in → state assessment → capability matching → surfaced invitations) or do they all stop at one piece?

## Specific Areas to Investigate

### 1. State-first / mood-first apps
- Apps where you log or assess your state *before* seeing your tasks or schedule
- "How are you feeling?" as an entry point that shapes what the app shows you
- Energy-based planning tools (plan around energy levels, not time slots)
- Body-budget or interoception-focused apps (beyond meditation apps)

### 2. ADHD/autism-specific life tools
- Beyond Tiimo, Routinery, Goblin Tools, Finch, Habitica — what else exists?
- Tools designed for executive function support in *life* (not just work)
- Tools that acknowledge capability fluctuation (good days/bad days) as a core feature
- Sensory regulation tools — apps that help with sensory environment management
- "Body doubling" apps or virtual co-working spaces

### 3. Coaching/companion apps
- AI-powered coaching apps that attempt ongoing attunement (not just one-off check-ins)
- Apps that take a "cognitive prosthesis" approach — extending the user's capacity rather than replacing their judgment
- Digital companions that learn the user's patterns over time
- Anything attempting the "trusted third person" role digitally

### 4. Failed or abandoned approaches
- Apps that tried state-first approaches and pivoted away — why?
- Neurodivergent-targeted apps that shut down — what can we learn?
- Features in mainstream apps (Apple Health, Google Fit, etc.) that attempted mood/state tracking and were underused
- Academic prototypes or research apps that never made it to market

### 5. Adjacent innovations
- Period/cycle tracking apps (Clue, Flo) as models for body-state-aware planning
- Chronic illness management apps (Bearable, Flaredown) that track state and correlate with activity
- Mental health apps (Daylio, Woebot) that combine check-ins with behavioral suggestions
- Smart home integration approaches that adapt environment to state

## Context for the Researcher

We're not trying to build an app — we're building an agent-based system where AI agents play the role of perceiver (builds a picture of the person's current state from voice notes, sensors, and signals) and attendant (acts on that picture — surfaces invitations, holds vigils on important things, manages logistics). The system could in theory be done with post-it notes and a trusted human partner taking the agent roles.

We want to understand the consumer landscape because:
- It tells us what problems real users prioritize
- Failures reveal where the hard problems are
- Successes we haven't found validate (or challenge) our assumptions
- The gap between what exists and what we're building is itself informative

## Desired Output

A structured landscape map organized by approach type. For each notable product or tool:
- Name, creator, current status (active/dead/pivoted)
- What approach it takes and which of our synthesis elements it covers
- User reception — does it actually work for people? (App store reviews, Reddit discussions, etc.)
- What it's missing relative to the full attunement-to-action loop

Conclude with: what gap in the market does our approach address that existing tools don't? Or does something already exist that we should learn from?
